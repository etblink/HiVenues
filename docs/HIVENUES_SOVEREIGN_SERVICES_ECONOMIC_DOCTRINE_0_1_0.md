# HiVenues Sovereign Services Economic Doctrine 0.1.0

Status: **FROZEN — accepted 2026-09-18**

## Purpose

This doctrine defines how HiVenues may create sustainable revenue without defeating the sovereignty, openness, portability, and Hive alignment that justify the product in the first place.

It governs economic design across future distribution, deployment, commerce, marketplace, support, funding, and treasury work.

It is subordinate to the existing product, journey, Hive-integration, distribution/deployment, and rendering/authoring doctrines. It does not reorder the strategic roadmap.

The governing economic architecture is:

**Sovereign Core + Paid Convenience + Hive-Aligned Upside**

The operating rule is:

**Monetize scarce services and real work, not artificial control of the software or the user.**

## Philosophical basis

This model is intentionally consistent with the economic and sovereignty principles described in The Digital Community Manifesto, especially:

- Chapter 12: useful DApps become long-term stakeholders in the base layer because stake supplies real network capacity;
- Chapter 20: open-source builders should rely less on proprietary IP rent and more on community/network effects and alignment with the base token;
- Chapter 23: public-good development can be funded through accountable decentralized community funding rather than founder-heavy pre-mines or compromising early capital;
- the book's Value-for-Value framing: people may voluntarily return value when they receive value.

The Manifesto is a philosophical north star, not a replacement for HiVenues' own frozen product doctrine or roadmap.

## 1. Sovereign core

The ordinary HiVenues software must remain usable without purchasing a HiVenues-managed service.

A nonpaying user must be able to retain, subject to ordinary technical prerequisites:

- access to the HiVenues source code;
- the official local Studio/distribution path;
- local authoring and durable workspace ownership;
- their own Hive identity and wallet authority;
- self-hosting or migration to a provider of their choice;
- export/backup paths sufficient to leave a HiVenues-managed service without forfeiting their work;
- the ability to continue operating from an independent fork where the open-source license permits.

Payment to HiVenues must never be the authority boundary for a user's Hive account, private keys, source content, HostGraph, Release History, imported media, domain ownership where the user owns the domain, right to self-host, or right to leave.

Cancellation of a paid HiVenues service must not silently destroy or confiscate user-owned state.

## 2. Primary recurring revenue — managed deployment and operations

The primary recurring commercial product should be **HiVenues Managed** or an equivalent first-party managed service.

Customers may pay HiVenues to perform operational work they are free to perform themselves or obtain elsewhere, including deployment, hosting coordination, VPS provisioning/orchestration, TLS and domain setup, backups, monitoring, upgrades, recovery, migration, diagnostics, operational support, and managed infrastructure capacity.

This is payment for competent service, convenience, reliability, and accountability.

It is not payment for permission to use HiVenues.

The deployment architecture must remain provider-neutral. A preferred/reference provider such as Privex may receive first-class integration without becoming a product-model dependency.

A customer who stops paying for managed operations must have a reasonable migration/export path instead of being trapped by an intentionally proprietary deployment format.

## 3. Professional services

HiVenues may charge market rates for real human or organizational work, including white-glove launch, brand and Direction work, content migration, custom integrations, custom components, accessibility remediation, training, priority support, enterprise/community onboarding, bespoke deployment or migration, incident response, and consulting.

The fact that the underlying software is open source does not make human labor, service guarantees, or expertise free.

## 4. Marketplace economics

A future HiVenues marketplace may support independent creators/providers offering Directions, components, integrations, deployment adapters, migration services, design services, host packs, and operational services.

HiVenues may charge a transparent marketplace/service fee when HiVenues actually provides marketplace value such as discovery, distribution, provenance, updates, payment facilitation, dispute tooling, or support.

A marketplace fee must be disclosed before the consequence.

The marketplace must not become the only technically permitted source of compatible extensions where an open extension mechanism exists.

## 5. No mandatory tax on peer-to-peer Hive value

HiVenues must not impose a mandatory percentage toll merely because a person uses a HiVenues interface to transfer value directly to another person or host on Hive.

Examples include tips, patron support, donations, direct HIVE/HBD transfers, host-native support actions, and other direct peer-to-peer value movements admitted by product doctrine.

The value belongs to the participating parties.

HiVenues may charge for optional services around commerce—such as managed storefront operations, accounting integrations, reporting, fulfillment integrations, marketplace services, or support—when those services create separate value.

The distinction is simple:

Peer-to-peer value transfer → no mandatory HiVenues tax.

HiVenues-provided commercial service → transparent service price may apply.

## 6. No user-data extraction model

HiVenues must not make sale of user/customer data, surveillance advertising, or undisclosed behavioral profiling a core revenue model.

Operational telemetry must remain bounded by legitimate product/security/support needs and applicable consent/privacy requirements.

A paid service must not be made artificially cheaper by converting the customer into the product without clear, informed agreement.

## 7. Hive-aligned treasury upside

HiVenues may deliberately accumulate HIVE/Hive Power as the product succeeds.

The purpose is alignment and utility, not a promise of speculative return.

A future treasury policy may allocate a portion of genuine business surplus—after taxes, operating obligations, appropriate reserves, and owner compensation—to HIVE/HP when prudent.

The intended flywheel is:

more useful HiVenues adoption
→ more real service revenue
→ stronger sustainable development
→ some treasury accumulation of HIVE/HP
→ more Hive stake/resource capacity and deeper ecosystem alignment
→ better ability to serve users on Hive.

No fixed percentage is frozen in this doctrine. Treasury allocation is an operational/financial policy that may change with business conditions.

HiVenues must not represent HIVE accumulation as a guaranteed investment return.

## 8. Value for Value

HiVenues may provide simple voluntary Value-for-Value support paths.

A user who receives value from the free software may voluntarily support the project through admitted payment rails such as HBD/HIVE or other appropriate methods.

Value-for-Value is supplementary revenue, not a gate to the sovereign core.

Voluntary support must not quietly become a required fee.

## 9. Decentralized public-good funding

HiVenues may seek Hive DHF/DAO or similar decentralized community funding for clearly defined public-good work that benefits the ecosystem.

Good candidates include open-source infrastructure, Hive onboarding capability, provider-neutral adapters, public interoperability, security work, accessibility, documentation, broad ecosystem integrations, and measurable adoption/public-good milestones.

Community funding should be milestone-bound, transparent, and accountable.

It should supplement a real customer/service economy rather than permanently substitute for one.

Community funding must not purchase exclusive control over user identity, user data, or the open-source core.

## 10. No HiVenues token by default

HiVenues does not currently need a separate token.

Therefore the default policy is:

- no HiVenues ICO;
- no founder pre-mine;
- no token sale as a development-financing shortcut;
- no speculative token merely because blockchain projects often issue one.

Hive already supplies identity, stake, resource capacity, governance, value rails, and HBD.

A future HiVenues-specific token would require a new explicit doctrine/roadmap decision demonstrating a real coordination or resource problem that cannot be solved cleanly with Hive or ordinary service economics.

Any future token design would have to address fair distribution, governance capture, legal/regulatory exposure, utility, and founder conflicts independently. This doctrine does not authorize one.

## 11. Open-source license policy

HiVenues adopts **GNU Affero General Public License version 3 or later (AGPL-3.0-or-later)** for HiVenues-owned software source unless a file or third-party component states another license.

The choice is deliberate.

AGPL preserves the right to use the software commercially, inspect it, modify it, redistribute it, fork it, and operate competing services.

Its reciprocal network-source obligation also means a party that modifies the AGPL-covered network-interactive program and offers that modified version to users over a network must preserve source availability as required by the license.

This is closer to HiVenues' economic doctrine than a permissive MIT/Apache default because improvements cannot be converted as easily into a closed hosted fork while the original community receives none of the modified source.

### AGPL is not a tollbooth

HiVenues does **not** adopt AGPL as the default half of an "open source unless you pay us for a proprietary exemption" business model.

The preferred commercial model is services, operations, expertise, marketplace value, voluntary support, public-good funding, and Hive alignment.

Commercial competitors may compete under AGPL.

Competition is allowed; removal of user software freedoms is not the business moat.

### Package-registry safety

The repository may retain "private": true in package.json.

That setting prevents accidental npm publication. It does not mean the repository is proprietary or negate the explicit AGPL license grant.

## 12. Official brand and trusted distribution

Open source does not require HiVenues to blur the distinction between an official upstream release and an unrelated fork.

The official HiVenues project may use the HiVenues name and visual identity, verified publisher identity, signed release artifacts, release provenance, official service domains, and reputation/history to identify first-party distributions and services.

Brand/trademark controls, if used, must not become a disguised way to prevent lawful forks, interoperability, truthful attribution, or self-hosting.

The economic moat should be trust, product quality, operations, reputation, community relationships, and service quality—not uncertainty about whether users are allowed to run the code.

## 13. Portability and exit are commercial requirements

A managed HiVenues customer must be able to understand what happens if they leave.

Commercial design should prefer customer-owned or transferable domains, exportable host/workspace data, portable media, documented backup/restore, provider-neutral deployment state where practical, explicit separation between user truth and HiVenues service metadata, and migration without ransom-like egress barriers.

Reasonable charges for actual migration labor or infrastructure usage are allowed.

Artificial lock-in fees whose primary purpose is to make exit uneconomic are not.

## 14. Pricing principles

Paid HiVenues offerings should price the value of service being delivered.

Acceptable pricing bases may include managed infrastructure tier, storage/bandwidth, backup/retention level, support response level, number or complexity of managed territories where operational cost scales, professional-service hours or scoped projects, and marketplace services actually rendered.

Pricing should be disclosed clearly before purchase.

HiVenues should avoid pricing mechanisms whose main effect is to appropriate value that the software did not create—for example, taking a percentage of every direct Hive donation simply because a button was rendered by HiVenues.

Free/self-hosted users must not be degraded merely to manufacture pain that the paid tier then removes.

## 15. Third-party licensing and binary release gate

The AGPL grant covers HiVenues-owned software; third-party dependencies retain their own licenses.

Before a broad binary release, the distribution pipeline must complete a reproducible third-party license/notices audit for everything actually shipped, including the redistributed private Node runtime.

The current package-lock metadata is not by itself sufficient as a legal-notices inventory because some transitive packages omit a license field even when their upstream packages carry licenses.

Broad release must therefore ensure that bundled third-party licenses/notices are preserved as required, Node's own license/third-party notices are included where required, generated installer/distributable materials do not falsely imply third-party code is AGPL-covered, the official product provides an obvious Source/License path appropriate for a network-interactive AGPL application, and release provenance ties binaries back to the corresponding public source.

This is a release-compliance gate, not a reason to delay licensing HiVenues-owned source now.

## 16. Economic acceptance test

Before admitting a new revenue mechanism, ask:

1. What real service or scarce work is the customer paying for?
2. Can a nonpaying user still retain their identity, keys, data, and self-hosting freedom?
3. Does cancellation permit exit without forfeiting user-owned state?
4. Are direct peer-to-peer Hive transfers left untaxed unless HiVenues is supplying a separate paid service?
5. Is the fee transparent before the consequence?
6. Does the mechanism avoid selling user data or manufacturing artificial lock-in?
7. Does it strengthen rather than weaken HiVenues' alignment with Hive and the open-source commons?
8. Could a competitor lawfully provide the same class of service under the project license?
9. If a new token is proposed, is there a real necessity that Hive and ordinary service economics cannot satisfy?
10. Would the model still feel legitimate if explained plainly to the customer on the purchase screen?

A revenue idea that fails these questions should not be admitted merely because it could increase short-term income.

## Frozen economic architecture

**Sovereign Core**
- free/open HiVenues software
- local-first ownership
- self-hosting
- portable identity/data
- no key custody

**Paid Convenience**
- managed deployment
- hosting/operations
- backups/monitoring/upgrades
- support
- professional services
- marketplace services

**Supplementary Funding**
- Value for Value
- DHF/DAO public-good proposals

**Hive-Aligned Upside**
- sustainable business surplus
- optional HIVE/HP treasury accumulation
- greater Hive resource capacity/alignment

The prohibited shortcut is:

user dependence
→ artificial lock-in
→ proprietary rent
→ mandatory transaction tax
→ data extraction
→ speculative founder token.

That is not the HiVenues business model.
